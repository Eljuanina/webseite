### Key takeaways

**Stéphane Hallegatte** stressed that climate change causes severe economic damage, above all in poorer countries, and that investing in adaptation can reduce these damages considerably. Renewables are already cheaper than fossil fuels in many places. The transition needs investment, its financing matters a great deal, and workers need training to move into new jobs.

**Prof. Laura Diaz Anadon** showed how policy-driven markets have brought down the cost of clean technologies: solar power in 2024 cost roughly a fifth of its 2014 level, wind roughly 40 percent and lithium-ion batteries around 13 percent. Not every country should produce everything; countries should focus on technologies where they have strengths and that matter for their policy goals.

**Prof. Ralph Ossa** argued that production emissions are usually much larger than transport emissions. If countries specialise in what they can produce cleanly and trade the rest, global emissions can fall. Around one third of the emission reductions from carbon pricing come through trade effects, so trade can be part of the solution.
